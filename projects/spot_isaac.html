Autonomous Indoor Navigation for Urban Search & Rescue

Developing legged robotic autonomy for GPS-denied, cluttered indoor environments encountered during disaster response.

Operational Context

Urban search and rescue operations often require entering damaged or unstable indoor environments where human access is unsafe or limited. Robots operating in these spaces must navigate without GPS, tolerate cluttered geometry, and maintain stable mobility under uncertainty.

  • Collapsed or partially damaged buildings
  • Narrow corridors, doorways, and stairwells
  • Debris and uneven indoor surfaces
  • GPS-denied localization
  • Limited lighting and constrained visibility

Robotic Capabilities

Stable Legged Locomotion Indoors

A reinforcement-learned locomotion policy enables smooth and stable quadruped walking in confined indoor environments without relying on hand-engineered gaits.

Autonomous Goal-Directed Navigation

The robot plans and executes paths to target locations while maintaining locomotion stability, enabling autonomous exploration and inspection.

Localization Without GPS

Localization is handled through onboard sensing and state estimation, allowing operation in GPS-denied indoor disaster scenarios.

Obstacle-Aware Motion Planning

Navigation accounts for obstacles and constrained spaces common in damaged indoor environments.

Autonomy Stack

The autonomy system combines learning-based and classical components, selected for reliability, interpretability, and rapid development.

Reinforcement learning accelerates the development of robust locomotion, while the ROS 2 Nav2 stack provides proven localization, planning, and control for indoor navigation tasks.

Constraints & Failure Modes

Several practical challenges were encountered during system integration and testing:

  • Timing and synchronization issues between sensors and localization
  • Sensor latency affecting navigation stability
  • Velocity command tuning for legged platforms
  • Navigation oscillations in narrow indoor spaces

These constraints highlight the importance of system-level debugging and careful integration when deploying autonomy in real-world disaster environments.
